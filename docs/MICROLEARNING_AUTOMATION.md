Update `content/microlearning/manifest.json` atomically in the same commit. Never remove an older edition or silently rewrite IDs of saved cards. ## Editorial direction

A pleasant alternative to a few minutes on Instagram. Discovery and delight matter: the hook should make the reader curious; a concrete example/image should make one idea comprehensible. Each card is complete, not a teaser that hides its answer. Use an image-led card: short title, central meaningful image, then roughly 35–65 words explaining the curiosity (up to 80 when essential). One or two short paragraphs; avoid repeating the title, caption and conclusion. Hook and takeaway remain metadata; the image-led renderer keeps them out of the main reading. Do not split an essay into twenty similar blocks. No academic voice, “the author says” commentary, repetitive disclaimers or generic motivational aphorisms. Put source context, editorial additions and necessary qualifications in `basis`; keep any scientifically essential uncertainty in the body as well.

Target approximately 12 technical cards and 8 broader-interest cards, with flexible proportions and interleaved topics rather than a rigid course checklist. Technical selection includes machine learning, CFD, FEM, computing architectures and concepts relevant to AI orchestration/JarvisOS and biotechnology companies. Cover ATP, CPPOM, APC, CCRE, PSE B and POCI only where a syllabus or actual course material verifies the connection. Do not expand acronyms, invent course contents or assume which elective courses the reader considered. Until course materials are available to this job, use actually read public primary sources for these technical areas, clearly treated as independent explorations rather than summaries of a particular lecture. Do not claim to have consulted private files, slides or programmes that the job cannot access. Candidate themes include numerical convergence versus physical correctness, mesh dependence, boundary conditions, transport and reaction, process control, optimization, model validation, ML generalization/data leakage, software concurrency, idempotency and AI tool governance. Rotate themes; do not force every card into a project application.

When course material is provided, select covered concepts and connect them to concrete engineering/biotech problems; use original explanations and examples, verified units, assumptions, validity ranges and limits. Never upload lecture PDFs, exam papers, private annotations or strategic project material to this public repository. Use materials only when actually accessible and authorized; ground public cards in independently accessible references without reproducing protected passages or private details. Missing materials must not be treated as permission to fabricate. Electives remain unassigned until their names/programmes are supplied.

The other roughly 8 cards span nature/ecology/biology, philosophy or scientific worldviews, books, history, human experience and judgement. These are directions, not quotas: avoid forcing technical usefulness on nature or literature. Draw from the public reading candidates in `docs/EDITORIAL_AUTOMATION.md` and `content/editorial/policy.json`, plus sources found through actual research. Add Stephen Wolfram, *What Ultimately Is There?... And Other Foundational Questions* (verified title and primary essays); Francesca Mastrogiacomi, *Empathy and Artificial Intelligence in Action: A Design Thinking Framework for Human-Centred Innovation* (publisher front matter and actually accessible sections). An index or marketing description does not count as reading a book. Independently authored examples must not be attributed to an author. Distinguish hypotheses, interpretations, fiction and established results without turning a card into disclaimers. Include nature regularly beyond its use for BlueRev.

Deepstash is a product reference for one idea per card, visual hierarchy, compact reading, source and personal collections. Do not copy its cards, proprietary assets or marketing claims. Use autonomous explanations and primary sources actually consulted, within copyright limits. Do not reconstruct copyrighted pages or imitate living authors' distinctive styles.

## Format and visuals

Read `src/microlearning.js` and `src/microlearning-view.js`. Contract: schema 1, date, exactly twenty cards for new editions (the validator also accepts the four-card historical pilot) with globally unique stable lowercase IDs, topic, title, hook, paragraphs, takeaway, basis, HTTPS sources, tone and visual. Allowed tones: sage, clay, plum, blue. Every NEW card must use visual.kind = "image" with fields src, alt, caption, credit, license, source. src is a checked-in file under `content/microlearning/media/` with a lowercase filename and extension svg/png/jpg/jpeg/webp, e.g. `content/microlearning/media/leaf-water.svg`. The archive renderer still accepts legacy templates only for old saved copies; do not generate new contrast/question icons. No decorative question marks, stock arrows or the same schematic pasted onto unrelated subjects.

The central image must teach something: a real photograph of the organism or object, a labelled mechanism, an accurate graph, a mesh, an annotated map or a faithful scientific illustration. Select the image with the idea, before writing the caption and compact explanation. Prefer photographs for natural organisms/objects and actual graphs or meaningful diagrams for quantitative mechanisms; vary the feed, do not turn every subject into a box-and-arrow flowchart. A diagram must show the mechanism/relationship, not merely repeat two textual labels. Search the web for relevant reusable photographs; verify the original source, author/credit and license (public access is not reuse permission). Download and optimize an authorized copy into this repository, without hotlinks or third-party trackers. Aim for <=250 KB per raster image; maximum 1 MB; keep SVG diagrams compact. Provide an accurate alt, concise caption, real credit and license, and an HTTPS source page in visual.source. Do not claim to have searched or inspected an image without doing so. If a suitable photo is unavailable use an original informative illustration, not a placeholder icon.

Original static SVG assets are allowed for precise diagrams, written as assets rather than injected markup: use a viewBox, readable labels on a 390px phone, explicit units/scales and a short indication of illustrative versus measured values. Never invent empirical curves; analytical examples must match the stated formula. No scripts, foreignObject, event handlers, external resources, embedded HTML, CSS imports or links inside SVG. Do not reproduce protected diagrams without permission. Do not add software or paid generation services from this job. The asset, JSON and manifest must be in the same commit. No raw HTML/SVG/JS in JSON fields.

Before publication check each actual image file: opens, depicts the stated subject, readable at phone width, no cropped diagram labels, photo not distorted, caption and explanation agree. Source and rights metadata live in expandable context, not large blocks in the feed. Validate local asset existence and image safety with `npm run validate:content`. If no image can support an idea, choose another verified idea rather than ship a text wall or generic pictogram.

Read at least 30 previous editions and compare concepts, not just titles. Avoid repeating the same idea or merely reducing the daily editorial to twenty cards. A later related idea should add a concrete new connection. A rerun must be a no-op when the target date is already present, unless the owner specifically requests a correction.
