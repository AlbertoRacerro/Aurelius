## Editorial direction

A pleasant alternative to a few minutes on Instagram. Discovery and delight matter: the hook should make the reader curious; a concrete example/image should make one idea comprehensible. Each card is complete, not a teaser that hides its answer. Prefer roughly 60–110 words of core reading, at most three short paragraphs, plus a brief hook and takeaway. Do not split an essay into twenty similar blocks. No academic voice, “the author says” commentary, repetitive disclaimers or generic motivational aphorisms. Put source context, editorial additions and necessary qualifications in `basis`; keep any scientifically essential uncertainty in the body as well.

Target approximately 12 technical cards and 8 broader-interest cards, with flexible proportions and interleaved topics rather than a rigid course checklist. Technical selection includes machine learning, CFD, FEM, computing architectures and concepts relevant to AI orchestration/JarvisOS and biotechnology companies. Cover ATP, CPPOM, APC, CCRE, PSE B and POCI only where a syllabus or actual course material verifies the connection. Do not expand acronyms, invent course contents or assume which elective courses the reader considered. Until course materials are available to this job, use actually read public primary sources for these technical areas, clearly treated as independent explorations rather than summaries of a particular lecture. Do not claim to have consulted private files, slides or programmes that the job cannot access. Candidate themes include numerical convergence versus physical correctness, mesh dependence, boundary conditions, transport and reaction, process control, optimization, model validation, ML generalization/data leakage, software concurrency, idempotency and AI tool governance. Rotate themes; do not force every card into a project application.

When course material is provided, select covered concepts and connect them to concrete engineering/biotech problems; use original explanations and examples, verified units, assumptions, validity ranges and limits. Never upload lecture PDFs, exam papers, private annotations or strategic project material to this public repository. Use materials only when actually accessible and authorized; ground public cards in independently accessible references without reproducing protected passages or private details. Missing materials must not be treated as permission to fabricate. Electives remain unassigned until their names/programmes are supplied.

The other roughly 8 cards span nature/ecology/biology, philosophy or scientific worldviews, books, history, human experience and judgement. These are directions, not quotas: avoid forcing technical usefulness on nature or literature. Draw from the public reading candidates in `docs/EDITORIAL_AUTOMATION.md` and `content/editorial/policy.json`, plus sources found through actual research. Add Stephen Wolfram, *What Ultimately Is There?... And Other Foundational Questions* (verified title and primary essays); Francesca Mastrogiacomi, *Empathy and Artificial Intelligence in Action: A Design Thinking Framework for Human-Centred Innovation* (publisher front matter and actually accessible sections). An index or marketing description does not count as reading a book. Independently authored examples must not be attributed to an author. Distinguish hypotheses, interpretations, fiction and established results without turning a card into disclaimers. Include nature regularly beyond its use for BlueRev.

Deepstash is a product reference for one idea per card, visual hierarchy, compact reading, source and personal collections. Do not copy its cards, proprietary assets or marketing claims. Use autonomous explanations and primary sources actually consulted, within copyright limits. Do not reconstruct copyrighted pages or imitate living authors' distinctive styles.
